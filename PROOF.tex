\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s asked whether, for every fixed \(r\ge3\), there is a constant
\(c_r>r^{-r}\) such that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), every
sufficiently large \(r\)-uniform hypergraph on \(N\) vertices with
at least \((1+\varepsilon)(N/r)^r\) edges contains
a subgraph on \(m\to\infty\) vertices with at least \(c_rm^r\) edges.
We give counterexamples for every \(r\ge5\). The base construction is
a sequence of explicit \(5\)-uniform hypergraphs \(G_n\) whose
unnormalized Lagrangians satisfy
\[
 5^{-5}\left(1+\frac1{4(3n)^{3}}\right)
 \le\lambda(G_n)\le5^{-5}+\frac1{4!n}.
\]
Blow-ups give the counterexample for \(r=5\), and adjoining common
vertices to every edge lifts it to all larger uniformities. Thus
Problem~1075, as a statement for all \(r\ge3\), has a negative answer.
The endpoint question remains open for \(3\le r\le4\), including the
case \(2/9\) for \(3\)-graphs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

All hypergraphs in this paper are finite and simple. For a hypergraph
\(H\), write \(v(H)=|V(H)|\), \(e(H)=|E(H)|\), and let \(H[S]\)
denote the subgraph induced by \(S\subseteq V(H)\).

Erd\H{o}s proved that, for every fixed \(r\ge2\) and \(\varepsilon>0\),
an \(r\)-uniform hypergraph on \(N\) vertices with at least
\(\varepsilon N^r\) edges contains a complete \(r\)-partite
\(r\)-uniform hypergraph with equal part sizes tending to infinity
with \(N\) \cite{Erdos64}. In particular, it contains a subgraph
on \(m\to\infty\) vertices with \((m/r)^r\) edges.
Erd\H{o}s subsequently conjectured that, for each fixed \(r\ge3\),
there is a constant \(c_r>r^{-r}\) such that, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\), the assumption
\[
 e(H)\ge(1+\varepsilon)(N/r)^r
\]
forces a subgraph on \(m=m(N)\to\infty\) vertices with at least
\(c_rm^r\) edges \cite[p.~80]{Erdos74}.
This is Problem~1075 in Bloom's collection \cite{Bloom}.

With the usual density \(d(H)=e(H)/\binom{v(H)}r\), the threshold
\((N/r)^r\) corresponds asymptotically to
\(\alpha_r=r!/r^r\). Erd\H{o}s proved that every number in
\([0,\alpha_r)\) is a jump for \(r\)-graphs \cite{Erdos71}. Thus
Problem~1075 is the endpoint question at \(\alpha_r\).
Frankl and R\"odl disproved the earlier jumping constant conjecture by
constructing non-jumps for every \(r\ge3\) \cite{FranklRodl84}.
Frankl, Peng, R\"odl and Talbot later showed that
\((5/2)r!/r^r\) is a non-jump for every \(r\ge3\) \cite{FPRT07},
and Peng proved the lifting theorem that carries a non-jump of the form
\(c\,r!/r^r\) to all larger uniformities \cite{Peng09}. Yan and Peng
proved that \(12/25\) is a non-jump for \(3\)-graphs, yielding
\((54/25)r!/r^r\) for every \(r\ge3\) by lifting \cite{YanPeng23}.

Shaw recently proved that \(2r!/r^r\) is a non-jump for every
\(r\ge4\), and that for \(r\ge4\) no smaller non-jump can be obtained
from his finite-pattern formulation of the Frankl--R\"odl method
\cite[Theorems~4.2 and~4.3]{Shaw26}. Liu and Mubayi subsequently proved
that \(4/9\) is a non-jump for \(3\)-graphs by a construction outside
that finite-pattern framework \cite{LiuMubayi26}. The endpoint
\(2/9\) for \(3\)-graphs remains open; Baber and Talbot proved, using
flag algebras, the first jump intervals immediately above it
\cite{BaberTalbot11}. The result below shows that the endpoint is a non-jump for
every \(r\ge5\); the cases \(3\le r\le4\) remain open.

The construction below is not an instance of Shaw's
finite-pattern criterion. Its distinguished links between the two
vertex families follow the matching \(A_iB_i\) and the shifted matching
\(B_iA_{i+1}\) around a cycle, and the proof controls the full
Lagrangian of a growing sequence \(G_n\). Hence Shaw's
Theorem~4.3 does not apply.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(\gamma>5^{-5}\), there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) and
there are arbitrarily large \(5\)-uniform hypergraphs \(H\) such that
\[
 e(H)\ge(1+\varepsilon)\bigl(v(H)/5\bigr)^{5},
 \qquad
 e(H[S])<\gamma|S|^{5}
 \quad\text{for every nonempty }S\subseteq V(H).
\]
\end{theorem}

The conclusion is stronger than the formulation of Problem~1075,
since the bound holds for every nonempty vertex subset.
Taking \(5^{-5}<\gamma<c_{5}\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
rules out any \(c_{5}>5^{-5}\). In the usual density normalization,
it also implies that \(5!/5^{5}\) is a non-jump. The proof uses
explicit finite hypergraphs whose Lagrangians approach \(5^{-5}\)
from above. We use the standard unnormalized Lagrangian
\cite{MotzkinStraus65,Keevash11},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrangian}
 P_H(x)=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\prod_{v\in e}x_v,
 \qquad
 \lambda(H)=\max_{\substack{x_v\ge0\\\sum_vx_v=1}}P_H(x),
\end{equation}
without the factor \(r!\).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sequence}
For every integer \(n\ge2\), the \(5\)-uniform hypergraph \(G_n\)
defined in Section~\ref{sec:construction} has \(2n+7\) vertices and
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
 5^{-5}\left(1+\frac1{4(3n)^{3}}\right)
 \le\lambda(G_n)\le5^{-5}+\frac1{4!n}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The mechanism is visible at the level of the cubic forms. After
normalizing the bottom mass, the balanced weighting on the closed cycle
has
\[
 b_i+c=B-b_i+D=a_{i+1}+c=A-a_{i+1}+D=\frac13,
\]
so both cubic forms attain \(1/27\) even with \(D>0\), while the
additional edge \(\{U,V\}\cup\D\) contributes a gain of order
\(D^{3}\). Deleting one pair \(A_k,B_k\) opens the cycle; the
terminal mismatch forces a loss of order \(D^3\) with a coefficient
large enough to absorb that gain. Consequently every open path \(T_L\) has Lagrangian
exactly \(5^{-5}\), whereas the closed cycle has Lagrangian slightly
above \(5^{-5}\).

\section{The cyclic construction}\label{sec:construction}

Fix \(n\ge2\). Take pairwise distinct vertices
\[
 A_i,B_i\quad(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z),\qquad C,U,V,
 \qquad W_1,\qquad D_1,\ldots,D_{3}.
\]
Put \(\W=\{W_1\}\) and
\(\D=\{D_1,\ldots,D_{3}\}\).
On the vertex set \(\{A_i,B_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\cup
\{C\}\cup\D\), define two three-uniform hypergraphs by
\begin{align*}
 E(H_1)&=\bigcup_i
 \bigl\{\{A_i,X,Y\}:X\in\{B_i,C\},\
 Y\in\{B_j:j\ne i\}\cup\D\bigr\},\\
 E(H_2)&=\bigcup_i
 \bigl\{\{B_i,X,Y\}:X\in\{A_{i+1},C\},\
 Y\in\{A_j:j\ne i+1\}\cup\D\bigr\}.
\end{align*}
All subscripts are read modulo \(n\). Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edges}
 \begin{split}
 E(G_n)={}&\bigl\{\W\cup\{U\}\cup e:e\in E(H_1)\bigr\}\\
 &\cup\bigl\{\W\cup\{V\}\cup e:e\in E(H_2)\bigr\}
 \cup\bigl\{\{U,V\}\cup\D\bigr\}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus \(G_n\) is a simple \(5\)-uniform hypergraph.

Use lower-case letters for the weights of the corresponding vertices,
and write
\[
 A=\sum_i a_i,\qquad B=\sum_i b_i,\qquad
 D=\sum_{j=1}^{3}d_j,\qquad \omega=w_1.
\]
The two cubic forms and the polynomial of \(G_n\) are
\begin{align}
 P_1&=\sum_i a_i(b_i+c)(B-b_i+D),\label{eq:p1}\\
 P_2&=\sum_i b_i(a_{i+1}+c)(A-a_{i+1}+D),\label{eq:p2}\\
 P_{G_n}&=\omega(uP_1+vP_2)+uv\prod_{j=1}^{3}d_j.
 \label{eq:polynomial}
\end{align}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower}
The lower bound in \eqref{eq:bounds} holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assign the weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 \begin{gathered}
 w_1=\frac1{5},\qquad u=v=\frac1{10},\qquad
 a_i=b_i=\frac1{5n},\\
 d_j=\frac1{15n},\qquad c=\frac{n-1}{5n}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Their sum is one. Moreover,
\[
 A=B=c+D=\frac1{5},\qquad
 b_i+c=B-b_i+D=a_{i+1}+c=A-a_{i+1}+D=\frac1{5}.
\]
Thus \(P_1=P_2=5^{-3}\). The first term in
\eqref{eq:polynomial} is \(5^{-5}\), and the second is
\[
 \frac1{100(15n)^{3}}
 =\frac{5^{-5}}{4(3n)^{3}}.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{A bound for open paths}\label{sec:paths}

The path estimate uses the following inequality for finite sequences.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:energy}
Let \(N\ge1\), and let \(x_0,\ldots,x_N\) be nonnegative real numbers
such that \(x_N=0\) and \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i\ge1/2\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sequence-energy}
 \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i(1-x_{i+1})^2\ge\frac29.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
 \psi(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 x-x^2/2,&0\le x\le1,\\
 1/2,&x>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
For all \(x,y\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:potential-step}
 \psi(x)-\psi(y)\le x(1-y)^2.
\end{equation}
For \(x,y\le1\), this follows from the identity
\[
 x(1-y)^2-\psi(x)+\psi(y)
 =\frac{(x-2y+y^2)^2+y(1-y)^2(2-y)}2\ge0.
\]
If \(y\ge1\), the left side of \eqref{eq:potential-step} is
nonpositive. If \(x>1>y\), it equals \((1-y)^2/2\), so the
inequality holds in this case as well.

If some \(x_j\ge1/3\), summing \eqref{eq:potential-step} from
\(j\) to \(N-1\) gives
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i(1-x_{i+1})^2
 \ge\psi(x_j)-\psi(0)\ge\frac5{18}>\frac29.
\]
Otherwise every \(x_i<1/3\), and the sum is at least
\(\frac49\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_i\ge2/9\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cubic}
Let \(L\ge1\), and let \(a_1,\ldots,a_L,b_1,\ldots,b_L,c,D\)
be nonnegative numbers. Put
\[
 A=\sum_{i=1}^L a_i,\qquad B=\sum_{i=1}^L b_i,
 \qquad a_{L+1}=0,
\]
and suppose that \(A+B+c+D=1\). Define \(P_1,P_2\) by
\eqref{eq:p1} and \eqref{eq:p2}, with sums over \(1\le i\le L\).
Then, for every \(s\in[0,1]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic-loss}
 sP_1+(1-s)P_2\le\frac1{27}-\frac{s(1-s)D^3}{10}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 \delta_A=A-\frac13,\qquad\delta_B=B-\frac13,
 \qquad S=|\delta_A|+|\delta_B|,
\]
and
\[
 \alpha=\frac{A+D-c}{2}=D+\delta_A+\frac{\delta_B}{2},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{B+D-c}{2}=D+\frac{\delta_A}{2}+\delta_B.
\]
Completing the square gives
\begin{align}
 P_1&=\frac{A(1-A)^2}{4}-\sum_i a_i(b_i-\beta)^2,
 \label{eq:square1}\\
 P_2&=\frac{B(1-B)^2}{4}-\sum_i b_i(a_{i+1}-\alpha)^2.
 \label{eq:square2}
\end{align}
Write
\[
 E=s\sum_i a_i(b_i-\beta)^2
 +(1-s)\sum_i b_i(a_{i+1}-\alpha)^2,
 \qquad
 \Delta=\frac1{27}-sP_1-(1-s)P_2.
\]
The exact identity
\[
 \Delta=\frac{s\delta_A^2(4/3-A)}4
 +\frac{(1-s)\delta_B^2(4/3-B)}4+E
\]
and the bounds \(A,B\le1-D\), \(D\le1\) imply
\[
 \Delta\ge\frac D3\bigl(s\delta_A^2+(1-s)\delta_B^2\bigr)+E.
\]
Indeed, \((4/3-A)/4\ge(1/3+D)/4\ge D/3\), and similarly for
\(B\). By the weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \Delta\ge\frac{s(1-s)DS^2}{3}+E.
\end{equation}

If \(S\ge11D/20\), then
\[
 \Delta\ge\frac{121}{1200}s(1-s)D^3
 \ge\frac{s(1-s)D^3}{10},
\]
as required. Suppose therefore that \(S<11D/20\). Since
\(D\le1/3+S\), we have
\[
 D\le\frac{20}{27},\qquad
 A+B\ge\frac23-S\ge\frac7{27}>\frac14,
 \qquad 0<D-S\le\alpha,\beta\le\frac12.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} to the sequence
\[
 \frac{a_1}{\alpha},\frac{b_1}{\beta},\ldots,
 \frac{a_L}{\alpha},\frac{b_L}{\beta},0.
\]
Its sum is \(A/\alpha+B/\beta\ge2(A+B)>1/2\).
Each successive pair \(x,y\) contributes to \(E\) a term
\(s\alpha\beta^2x(1-y)^2\) or
\((1-s)\beta\alpha^2x(1-y)^2\). Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-loss}
 E\ge\frac29\min\{s\alpha\beta^2,(1-s)\beta\alpha^2\}
 \ge\frac29s(1-s)(D-S)^3.
\end{equation}

For completeness, for all \(S,m\ge0\) we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorption}
 \frac{(S+m)S^2}{3}+\frac{2m^3}{9}\ge\frac{(S+m)^3}{10}.
\end{equation}
Multiplying the difference by \(90\) gives the nonnegative expression
\begin{align*}
 &21S^3+3S^2m-27Sm^2+11m^3\\
 &\quad=21\left(S-\frac{3m}{5}\right)^2\left(S+\frac{6m}{5}\right)
 +3m\left(S-\frac{18m}{25}\right)^2+\frac{233m^3}{625}.
\end{align*}
Taking \(m=D-S\) in \eqref{eq:absorption} and using
\eqref{eq:loss}--\eqref{eq:energy-loss} proves \eqref{eq:cubic-loss}.
\end{proof}

For \(L\ge1\), define the \(5\)-uniform hypergraph \(T_L\)
as in \eqref{eq:edges}, with each index ranging over \(1,\ldots,L\)
and with the last link in \(H_2\) replaced by the triples
\[
 \{B_L,C,Y\},\qquad Y\in\{A_1,\ldots,A_L\}\cup\D.
\]
The edge \(\{U,V\}\cup\D\) is retained.
Its polynomial is \eqref{eq:polynomial} with \(a_{L+1}=0\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:path}
For every \(L\ge1\), we have \(\lambda(T_L)=5^{-5}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound follows by assigning weight \(1/5\) to the vertices
of any one edge and zero elsewhere. For the upper bound, take
nonnegative vertex weights of total mass one, and put
\[
 h=w_1,\qquad q=u+v,\qquad t=A+B+c+D.
\]
Thus \(h+q+t=1\). If \(q=0\) or \(t=0\), then \(P_{T_L}=0\).
Otherwise put
\[
 s=u/q,\qquad d=D/t,\qquad z=s(1-s)d^3\in[0,1/4].
\]
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic} to the bottom weights divided by
\(t\), and then using homogeneity, gives
\[
 uP_1+vP_2\le qt^3\left(\frac1{27}-\frac z{10}\right).
\]
The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality now yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-first}
 P_{T_L}\le
 hqt^3
       \left(\frac1{27}-\frac z{10}\right)
 +q^2s(1-s)\left(\frac{td}{3}\right)^3.
\end{equation}
Also,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amgm}
 \frac{qt^3}{27}\le\left(\frac{q+t}{4}\right)^4,
 \qquad
 \frac{q^2t^{3}}{3^{3}}\le4\left(\frac{q+t}{5}\right)^5.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 F(h)=\frac{3125}{256}h(1-h)^4.
\]
We have \(F(h)\le1\), with equality at \(h=1/5\).
Since \(1-27z/10>0\), we may substitute \eqref{eq:amgm}
into \eqref{eq:path-first}. We get
\begin{align}
 \frac{P_{T_L}}{5^{-5}}
 &\le F(h)\left(1-\frac{27}{10}z\right)
        +4s(1-s)d^{3}(1-h)^5\notag\\
 &=F(h)+z\left[4(1-h)^5-\frac{27}{10}F(h)\right].
 \label{eq:scalar}
\end{align}

It remains to show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-endpoint}
 \left(1-\frac{27}{40}\right)F(h)+(1-h)^5\le1
 \qquad(0\le h\le1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the last expression in \eqref{eq:scalar} is the convex
combination
\[
 (1-4z)F(h)+4z\left[\left(1-\frac{27}{40}\right)F(h)+(1-h)^5\right].
\]

Since \((1-27/40)(3125/256)=8125/2048<5\), the binomial
expansion gives
\[
 \left(1-\frac{27}{40}\right)F(h)+(1-h)^5
 \le5h(1-h)^4+(1-h)^5
 \le\bigl(h+(1-h)\bigr)^5=1.
\]
This completes the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{The Lagrangian bound and the blow-ups}\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sequence}]
The lower bound was proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:lower}.
For the upper bound, take any nonnegative weights on \(G_n\) of
total mass one. Choose an index \(k\) with
\[
 a_k+b_k\le\frac{A+B}{n}\le\frac1n.
\]
Put \(a'_j=a_{k+j}\), \(b'_j=b_{k+j}\) for
\(1\le j\le n-1\), with indices modulo \(n\), and set
\(A'=\sum_j a'_j\), \(B'=\sum_j b'_j\), \(a'_n=0\).
After deleting \(A_k,B_k\), the remaining polynomial is
\begin{align}
 P_{G_n-\{A_k,B_k\}}
 ={}&\omega u\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}
 a'_j(b'_j+c)(B'-b'_j+D) \notag\\
 &+\omega v\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}
 b'_j(a'_{j+1}+c)(A'-a'_{j+1}+D)
 +uv\prod_{\ell=1}^{3}d_\ell \notag\\
 ={}&P_{T_{n-1}}.
 \label{eq:deletion-identity}
\end{align}
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:path} and homogeneity bound the total weight of the
edges that remain by
\[
 5^{-5}(1-a_k-b_k)^{5}\le5^{-5}.
\]

For any simple \(5\)-uniform hypergraph and weights of total
mass one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivative}
 \frac{\partial P_H}{\partial x_v}
 \le\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V(H)\setminus\{v\}\\|S|=4}}
        \prod_{w\in S}x_w
 \le\frac{(\sum_{w\ne v}x_w)^{4}}{4!}
 \le\frac1{4!}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, each product of four distinct weights occurs \(4!\)
times in the power expansion, and all other terms are nonnegative.
The deleted edges consequently have total weight at most
\[
 a_k\frac{\partial P_{G_n}}{\partial a_k}
 +b_k\frac{\partial P_{G_n}}{\partial b_k}
 \le\frac{a_k+b_k}{4!}\le\frac1{4!n}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:bounds}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix \(\gamma>5^{-5}\), and choose an integer \(n\ge2\) such that
\[
 5^{-5}+\frac1{4!n}<\gamma.
\]
Keep \(n\) fixed, and set
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac1{4(3n)^{3}}>0.
\]
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
For each positive integer \(M\), form a blow-up \(B_{n,M}\) of
\(G_n\): replace each vertex by an independent class, and each
edge by all transversal edges across its five classes.
Use the following class sizes, with one class for each listed vertex:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \text{vertex}&W_j&U,V&A_i,B_i&C&D_j\\ \hline
 \text{class size}&6nM&3nM&6M&6(n-1)M&2M
 \end{array}.
\]
There are \(N=30nM\) vertices. These class sizes are proportional
to the weights in \eqref{eq:weights}, and the computation in
Lemma~\ref{lem:lower} gives the exact edge count
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blowup-count}
 e(B_{n,M})=\bigl[(6n)^{5}+72n^2\bigr]M^{5}
 =(1+\varepsilon)(N/5)^{5}.
\end{equation}

Let \(S\) be any nonempty set of \(m\) vertices of \(B_{n,M}\),
and let \(k_v\) be the number of its vertices in the class
corresponding to \(v\in V(G_n)\). Then
\[
 e(B_{n,M}[S])
 =\sum_{e\in E(G_n)}\prod_{v\in e}k_v
 =m^{5}P_{G_n}\bigl((k_v/m)_v\bigr)
 \le m^{5}\lambda(G_n)<\gamma m^{5}.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:blowup-count} and \(M\to\infty\), this
proves the theorem. A non-induced subgraph has no more edges than
the induced subgraph on the same vertex set.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:all-r}
For every integer \(r\ge5\) and every \(\gamma>r^{-r}\), there is an
\(\varepsilon>0\) and there are arbitrarily large \(r\)-uniform
hypergraphs \(H\) such that
\[
 e(H)\ge(1+\varepsilon)\bigl(v(H)/r\bigr)^r,
 \qquad
 e(H[S])<\gamma|S|^r
 \quad\text{for every nonempty }S\subseteq V(H).
\]
In particular, Problem~1075 fails for every \(r\ge5\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the lifting step of Peng \cite{Peng09}; in the present
normalization it has the following direct form. Let \(q=r-5\). For a
\(5\)-uniform hypergraph \(G\), form an
\(r\)-uniform hypergraph \(G^{+q}\) by adjoining the same set of
\(q\) new vertices to every edge. If the total weight on \(V(G)\) is
\(t\), then the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 P_{G^{+q}}\le \lambda(G)t^{5}
 \left(\frac{1-t}{q}\right)^q,
\]
with the usual interpretation when \(q=0\). The right side is
maximized at \(t=5/r\), and equality is attained by taking an optimal
weighting of \(G\), scaled by \(5/r\), and weight \(1/r\) on each new
vertex. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:suspension}
 \lambda(G^{+q})=\frac{5^{5}}{r^r}\lambda(G).
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:suspension} to \(G_n\) and using
Proposition~\ref{prop:sequence} gives
\[
 r^{-r}\left(1+\frac1{4(3n)^{3}}\right)
 \le \lambda(G_n^{+q})
 \le r^{-r}+\frac{5^{5}}{r^r4!n}.
\]
Choose \(n\) so that the upper bound is less than \(\gamma\).
The blow-up argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, using the
rational weighting obtained by suspending \eqref{eq:weights}, then
gives the stated \(r\)-uniform hypergraphs with
\(\varepsilon=1/[4(3n)^{3}]\).
\end{proof}
